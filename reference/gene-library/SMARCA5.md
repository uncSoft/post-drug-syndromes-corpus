# SMARCA5: SNF2 related chromatin remodeling ATPase 5

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 4 (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene is a member of the SWI/SNF family of proteins. Members of this family have helicase and ATPase activities and are thought to regulate transcription of certain genes by altering the chromatin structure around those genes.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** SWI/SNF-related matrix-associated actin-dependent regulator of chromatin subfamily A member 5 ([O60264](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O60264/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** ATPase that possesses intrinsic ATP-dependent nucleosome-remodeling activity. Catalytic subunit of ISWI chromatin-remodeling complexes, which form ordered nucleosome arrays on chromatin and facilitate access to DNA during DNA-templated processes such as DNA replication, transcription, and repair; this may require intact histone H4 tails. Within the ISWI chromatin-remodeling complexes, slides edge- and center-positioned histone octamers away from their original location on the DNA template. Catalytic activity and histone octamer sliding propensity is regulated and determined by components of the ISWI chromatin-remodeling complexes. The BAZ1A/ACF1-, BAZ1B/WSTF-, BAZ2A/TIP5- and BAZ2B-containing ISWI chromatin-remodeling complexes regulate the spacing of nucleosomes along the chromatin and have the ability to slide mononucleosomes to the center of a DNA template in an ATP-dependent manner.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Ubiquitously expressed.
