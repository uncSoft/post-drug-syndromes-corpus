# RARB: retinoic acid receptor beta

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 3 (steroid and nuclear hormone receptors). Encodes retinoic acid receptor beta, a member of the thyroid-steroid hormone receptor superfamily of nuclear transcriptional regulators. This receptor localizes to the cytoplasm and to subnuclear compartments.

**Corpus relevance:** Nuclear receptors transduce steroid signals into lasting gene-expression programs; persistent receptor-level remodeling (or silencing) is a leading hypothesis for symptoms that outlast drug exposure.

**Protein (UniProt):** Retinoic acid receptor beta ([P10826](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P10826/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Receptor for retinoic acid. Retinoic acid receptors bind as heterodimers to their target response elements in response to their ligands, all-trans or 9-cis retinoic acid, and regulate gene expression in various biological processes. The RXR/RAR heterodimers bind to the retinoic acid response elements (RARE) composed of tandem 5'-AGGTCA-3' sites known as DR1-DR5. In the absence or presence of hormone ligand, acts mainly as an activator of gene expression due to weak binding to corepressors. The RXRA/RARB heterodimer can act as a repressor on the DR1 element and as an activator on the DR5 element. In concert with RARG, required for skeletal growth, matrix homeostasis and growth plate function (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Cytoplasm.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Expressed in aortic endothelial cells (at protein level).

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Microphthalmia, syndromic, 12 (MCOPS12).
