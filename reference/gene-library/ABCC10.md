# ABCC10: ATP binding cassette subfamily C member 10

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 6 (drug and xenobiotic transporters). The protein encoded by this gene is a member of the superfamily of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters. ABC proteins transport various molecules across extra- and intra-cellular membranes.

**Corpus relevance:** Transporters set systemic exposure to finasteride/SSRIs and clear conjugated steroid metabolites, directly relevant to both drug pharmacokinetics and Powers' clearance-centered PFS mechanism.

**Powers note (his unpublished theorizing, research only, not medical advice):** Powers named the ABCC transporter family (2026 summit interview, unpublished) as intracellular transporter genes implicated in metabolite clearance in his experimentally verifiable mechanism; individual ABCC members not separately named.

**Protein (UniProt):** ATP-binding cassette sub-family C member 10 ([Q5T3U5](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q5T3U5/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** ATP-dependent transporter of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) family that actively extrudes physiological compounds, and xenobiotics from cells. Lipophilic anion transporter that mediates ATP-dependent transport of glucuronide conjugates such as estradiol-17-beta-o-glucuronide and GSH conjugates such as leukotriene C4 (LTC4). May regulate the transport of organic compounds in testes across the blood-testis-barrier (Probable). Mediates intercellular propagation of antiviral immune signaling in early stages of infection (By similarity). In RNA virus-infected cells, oligoadenylate synthase senses viral dsRNA and generates 2',5'-oligoadenylates (2-5A) which act as second messengers to activate RNASEL and type I interferon signaling to inhibit viral replication (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cell membrane; Basolateral cell membrane; Basal cell membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** In testis, localized to peritubular myoid cells, Leydig cells, along the basal membrane of Sertoli cells, moderately in the adluminal compartment of the seminiferous tubules, and in vascular endothelial cells.
