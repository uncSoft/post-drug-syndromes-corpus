# UGT2B10: UDP glucuronosyltransferase family 2 member B10

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 4 (steroid conjugation and clearance). Predicted to enable UDP-glycosyltransferase activity. Predicted to be involved in estrogen metabolic process.

**Corpus relevance:** Steroid clearance is the exit route for androgens and their metabolites, the pathway at the center of Powers' PFS model (defective glucuronidation/sulfation trapping androgens intracellularly). Gut bacterial enzymes can undo this clearance, linking it to the microbiome thread.

**Powers note (his unpublished theorizing, research only, not medical advice):** Covered by Powers' general claim (DWP-002, unpublished) that defective glucuronidation underlies his PFS phenotype (>50% of his PFS patients show ~zero urinary androgens on DUTCH testing); not individually named by him.

**Protein (UniProt):** UDP-glucuronosyltransferase 2B10 ([P36537](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P36537/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** UDP-glucuronosyltransferase (UGT) that catalyzes phase II biotransformation reactions in which lipophilic substrates are conjugated with glucuronic acid to increase the metabolite's water solubility, thereby facilitating excretion into either the urine or bile. Specialized in N-glucuronidation of tertiary amine-containing drugs and xenobiotics. Catalyzes N-glucuronidation of tobacco alkaloids, tobacco-specific nitrosamines, tricyclic antidepressants and compounds containing imidazole groups. Major enzyme responsible for detoxification of tobacco-specific nitrosamines. Shows strong preference for tertiary aliphatic and cyclic amines over secondary or primary amines. Does not glucuronidate steroids, hyodeoxycholic acid, or phenolic xenobiotics.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Microsome membrane; Endoplasmic reticulum membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Highly expressed in liver, not detected in other tissues.
