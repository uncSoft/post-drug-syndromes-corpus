# LDLR: low density lipoprotein receptor

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 19 (lipid and cholesterol homeostasis). The low density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) gene family consists of cell surface proteins involved in receptor-mediated endocytosis of specific ligands. The protein is normally bound at the cell membrane, where it binds low density lipoprotein/cholesterol and is taken into the cell.

**Corpus relevance:** Cholesterol is the obligate precursor of all steroid hormones; lipid handling sets substrate supply for steroidogenesis and neurosteroid synthesis.

**Protein (UniProt):** Low-density lipoprotein receptor ([P01130](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P01130/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Binds low density lipoprotein /LDL, the major cholesterol-carrying lipoprotein of plasma, and transports it into cells by endocytosis. In order to be internalized, the receptor-ligand complexes must first cluster into clathrin-coated pits. Forms a ternary complex with PGRMC1 and TMEM97 receptors which increases LDLR-mediated LDL internalization.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cell membrane; Membrane, clathrin-coated pit; Golgi apparatus; Early endosome; Late endosome; Lysosome.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Hypercholesterolemia, familial, 1 (FHCL1).
