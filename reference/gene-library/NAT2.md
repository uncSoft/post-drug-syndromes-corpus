# NAT2: N-acetyltransferase 2

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 8 (drug metabolism (phase i)). Encodes an enzyme that functions to both activate and deactivate arylamine and hydrazine drugs and carcinogens. Polymorphisms in this gene are responsible for the N-acetylation polymorphism in which human populations segregate into rapid, intermediate, and slow acetylator phenotypes.

**Corpus relevance:** Phase I enzymes determine exposure to finasteride, SSRIs, and other implicated drugs; variation here shapes the likelihood and persistence of adverse effects.

**Protein (UniProt):** Arylamine N-acetyltransferase 2 ([P11245](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P11245/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Catalyzes the N- or O-acetylation of various arylamine and heterocyclic amine substrates. Participates in the detoxification of a plethora of hydrazine and arylamine drugs, and is able to bioactivate several known carcinogens.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cytoplasm.
