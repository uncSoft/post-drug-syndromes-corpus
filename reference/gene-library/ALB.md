# ALB: albumin

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 4 (hpg and gonadal axis). Encodes the most abundant protein in human blood. This protein functions in the regulation of blood plasma colloid osmotic pressure and acts as a carrier protein for a wide range of endogenous molecules including hormones, fatty acids, and metabolites, as well as exogenous drugs.

**Corpus relevance:** HPG-axis signaling and steroid carrier proteins govern testosterone production and bioavailability. Whether this axis is disturbed in post-drug syndromes is unsettled: the library holds no LH or FSH measurement in men with persistent symptoms after finasteride or an SSRI, average testosterone and DHT were normal in the controlled study (SIDE-010), and 13% of 24 men in a smaller series had confirmed low levels of both (SIDE-008).

**Protein (UniProt):** Albumin ([P02768](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P02768/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Binds water, Ca(2+), Na(+), K(+), fatty acids, hormones, bilirubin and drugs (Probable). Its main function is the regulation of the colloidal osmotic pressure of blood (Probable). Major zinc transporter in plasma, typically binds about 80% of all plasma zinc. Major calcium and magnesium transporter in plasma, binds approximately 45% of circulating calcium and magnesium in plasma (By similarity). Potentially has more than two calcium-binding sites and might additionally bind calcium in a non-specific manner (By similarity). The shared binding site between zinc and calcium at residue Asp-273 suggests a crosstalk between zinc and calcium transport in the blood (By similarity). The rank order of affinity is zinc > calcium > magnesium (By similarity). Binds to the bacterial siderophore enterobactin and inhibits enterobactin-mediated iron uptake of E.coli from ferric transferrin, and may thereby limit the utilization of iron and growth of enteric bacteria such as E.coli.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Secreted.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Plasma.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Hyperthyroxinemia, familial dysalbuminemic (FDAH); Analbuminemia (ANALBA).
