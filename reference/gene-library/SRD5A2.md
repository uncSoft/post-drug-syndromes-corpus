# SRD5A2: steroid 5 alpha-reductase 2

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 2 (steroid hormone synthesis). Encodes a microsomal protein expressed at high levels in androgen-sensitive tissues such as the prostate. The protein is active at acidic pH and is sensitive to the 4-azasteroid inhibitor finasteride.

**Corpus relevance:** Steroid-synthesis enzymes sit directly on the pathways perturbed by 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors and SSRIs; inherited variation here is a candidate susceptibility factor for persistent post-drug phenotypes.

**Protein (UniProt):** 3-oxo-5-alpha-steroid 4-dehydrogenase 2 ([P31213](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P31213/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Catalyzes the irreversible stereospecific reduction of the delta 4,5 bond (double bond between carbons 4 and 5) of various 3-oxo steroids (3-keto steroids) producing their 5alpha dihydro-3-oxo forms. Converts testosterone into 5-alpha-dihydrotestosterone (DHT), the most active androgen in the prostate, as it is the preferred ligand for androgen receptor (AR) transactivation, making this reaction a key step in male sexual differentiation during development. Besides testosterone, it can also act on other steroids, including progesterone, producing metabolites with diverse roles. Hence, it plays a central role in sexual differentiation and androgen physiology.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Microsome membrane; Endoplasmic reticulum membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Expressed in high levels in the prostate and many other androgen-sensitive tissues.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Pseudovaginal perineoscrotal hypospadias (PPSH).
