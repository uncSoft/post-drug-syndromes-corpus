# SHBG: sex hormone binding globulin

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 17 (hpg and gonadal axis). Encodes a steroid binding protein that was first described as a plasma protein secreted by the liver but is now thought to participate in the regulation of steroid responses. The protein transports androgens and estrogens in the blood, binding each steroid molecule as a dimer formed from identical or nearly identical monomers.

**Corpus relevance:** HPG-axis signaling and steroid carrier proteins govern testosterone production and bioavailability. Whether this axis is disturbed in post-drug syndromes is unsettled: the library holds no LH or FSH measurement in men with persistent symptoms after finasteride or an SSRI, average testosterone and DHT were normal in the controlled study (SIDE-010), and 13% of 24 men in a smaller series had confirmed low levels of both (SIDE-008).

**Protein (UniProt):** Sex hormone-binding globulin ([P04278](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P04278/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Functions as an androgen transport protein, but may also be involved in receptor mediated processes. Each dimer binds one molecule of steroid. Specific for 5-alpha-dihydrotestosterone, testosterone, and 17-beta-estradiol. Regulates the plasma metabolic clearance rate of steroid hormones by controlling their plasma concentration.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Secreted.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Isoform 1 and isoform 2 are present in liver and testis.
