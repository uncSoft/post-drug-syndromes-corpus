# TACR3: tachykinin receptor 3

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 4 (hpg and gonadal axis). This gene belongs to a family of genes that function as receptors for tachykinins. Receptor affinities are specified by variations in the 5'-end of the sequence.

**Corpus relevance:** HPG-axis signaling and steroid carrier proteins govern testosterone production and bioavailability. Whether this axis is disturbed in post-drug syndromes is unsettled: the library holds no LH or FSH measurement in men with persistent symptoms after finasteride or an SSRI, average testosterone and DHT were normal in the controlled study (SIDE-010), and 13% of 24 men in a smaller series had confirmed low levels of both (SIDE-008).

**Protein (UniProt):** Neuromedin-K receptor ([P29371](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P29371/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Receptor for the tachykinin neuromedin-K (neurokinin B), also able to bind and respond to tachynins substance K/neurokinin A and substance P. The rank order of affinity of this receptor to tachykinins is: neuromedin-K > substance K and substance P. Neuromedin-K binding to its receptor triggers G protein-coupled receptor signaling via activation of G(q) and phosphatidylinositol hydrolysis by phospholipase C. Neuromedin-K binding also triggers signaling via activation of adenylate cyclase activity which results in increased intracellular levels of cyclic AMP (cAMP) (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cell membrane.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism 11 with or without anosmia (HH11).
