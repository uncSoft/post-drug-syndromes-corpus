# SIRT5: sirtuin 5

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 6 (epigenetic regulation). Encodes a member of the sirtuin family of proteins, homologs to the yeast Sir2 protein. Members of the sirtuin family are characterized by a sirtuin core domain and grouped into four classes.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** NAD-dependent protein deacylase sirtuin-5, mitochondrial ([Q9NXA8](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9NXA8/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** NAD-dependent lysine demalonylase, desuccinylase and deglutarylase that specifically removes malonyl, succinyl and glutaryl groups on target proteins. Activates CPS1 and contributes to the regulation of blood ammonia levels during prolonged fasting: acts by mediating desuccinylation and deglutarylation of CPS1, thereby increasing CPS1 activity in response to elevated NAD levels during fasting. Activates SOD1 by mediating its desuccinylation, leading to reduced reactive oxygen species. Activates SHMT2 by mediating its desuccinylation. Modulates ketogenesis through the desuccinylation and activation of HMGCS2 (By similarity). Has weak NAD-dependent protein deacetylase activity; however this activity may not be physiologically relevant in vivo. Can deacetylate cytochrome c (CYCS) and a number of other proteins in vitro such as UOX.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Mitochondrion matrix; Mitochondrion intermembrane space; Cytoplasm, cytosol; Nucleus; Cytoplasm; Mitochondrion.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Widely expressed.
