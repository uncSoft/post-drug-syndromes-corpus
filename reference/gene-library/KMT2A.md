# KMT2A: lysine methyltransferase 2A

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 11 (epigenetic regulation). Encodes a transcriptional coactivator that plays an essential role in regulating gene expression during early development and hematopoiesis. The protein contains multiple conserved functional domains.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2A ([Q03164](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q03164/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Histone methyltransferase that plays an essential role in early development and hematopoiesis. Catalytic subunit of the MLL1/MLL complex, a multiprotein complex that mediates both methylation of 'Lys-4' of histone H3 (H3K4me) complex and acetylation of 'Lys-16' of histone H4 (H4K16ac). Catalyzes methyl group transfer from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to the epsilon-amino group of 'Lys-4' of histone H3 (H3K4) via a non-processive mechanism. Part of chromatin remodeling machinery predominantly forms H3K4me1 and H3K4me2 methylation marks at active chromatin sites where transcription and DNA repair take place. Has weak methyltransferase activity by itself, and requires other component of the MLL1/MLL complex to obtain full methyltransferase activity. Has no activity toward histone H3 phosphorylated on 'Thr-3', less activity toward H3 dimethylated on 'Arg-8' or 'Lys-9', while it has higher activity toward H3 acetylated on 'Lys-9'.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Heart, lung, brain and T- and B-lymphocytes.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Wiedemann-Steiner syndrome (WDSTS).
