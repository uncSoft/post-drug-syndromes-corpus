# ALDH1A1: aldehyde dehydrogenase 1 family member A1

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 9 (drug metabolism (phase i)). The protein encoded by this gene belongs to the aldehyde dehydrogenase family. Aldehyde dehydrogenase is the next enzyme after alcohol dehydrogenase in the major pathway of alcohol metabolism.

**Corpus relevance:** Phase I enzymes determine exposure to finasteride, SSRIs, and other implicated drugs; variation here shapes the likelihood and persistence of adverse effects.

**Protein (UniProt):** Aldehyde dehydrogenase 1A1 ([P00352](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P00352/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Cytosolic dehydrogenase that catalyzes the NAD(+)-dependent irreversible oxidation of a wide range of aldehydes to their corresponding carboxylic acid. Functions downstream of retinol dehydrogenases and catalyzes the oxidation of retinaldehyde into retinoic acid, the second step in the oxidation of retinol/vitamin A into retinoic acid. This pathway is crucial to control the levels of retinol and retinoic acid, two important molecules which excess can be teratogenic and cytotoxic. Also oxidizes aldehydes resulting from lipid peroxidation like (E)-4-hydroxynon-2-enal/HNE, malonaldehyde and hexanal that form protein adducts and are highly cytotoxic. By participating for instance to the clearance of (E)-4-hydroxynon-2-enal/HNE in the lens epithelium prevents the formation of HNE-protein adducts and lens opacification.

**Pathway (UniProt):** Cofactor metabolism; retinol metabolism (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cytoplasm, cytosol; Cell projection, axon.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Expressed by erythrocytes (at protein level).
