# KMT2D: lysine methyltransferase 2D

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 12 (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene is a histone methyltransferase that methylates the Lys-4 position of histone H3. The protein is part of a large protein complex called ASCOM, which has been shown to be a transcriptional regulator of the beta-globin and estrogen receptor genes.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2D ([O14686](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O14686/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Histone methyltransferase that catalyzes methyl group transfer from S-adenosyl-L-methionine to the epsilon-amino group of 'Lys-4' of histone H3 (H3K4). Part of chromatin remodeling machinery predominantly forms H3K4me1 methylation marks at active chromatin sites where transcription and DNA repair take place. Acts as a coactivator for estrogen receptor by being recruited by ESR1, thereby activating transcription.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Expressed in most adult tissues, including a variety of hematoipoietic cells, with the exception of the liver.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Kabuki syndrome 1 (KABUK1); Branchial arch abnormalities, choanal atresia, athelia, hearing loss, and hypothyroidism syndrome (BCAHH).
