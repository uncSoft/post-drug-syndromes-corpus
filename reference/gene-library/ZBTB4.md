# ZBTB4: zinc finger and BTB domain containing 4

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 17 (epigenetic regulation). Enables several functions, including DNA-binding transcription repressor activity, RNA polymerase II-specific; nucleotide binding activity; and protein homodimerization activity. Involved in DNA damage response and negative regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase II.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Zinc finger and BTB domain-containing protein 4 ([Q9P1Z0](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9P1Z0/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Transcriptional repressor with bimodal DNA-binding specificity. Represses transcription in a methyl-CpG-dependent manner. Binds with a higher affinity to methylated CpG dinucleotides in the consensus sequence 5'-CGCG-3' but can also bind to the non-methylated consensus sequence 5'-CTGCNA-3' also known as the consensus kaiso binding site (KBS). Can also bind specifically to a single methyl-CpG pair and can bind hemimethylated DNA but with a lower affinity compared to methylated DNA. Plays a role in postnatal myogenesis, may be involved in the regulation of satellite cells self-renewal (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome.
