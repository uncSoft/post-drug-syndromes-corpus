# CHD5: chromodomain helicase DNA binding protein 5

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 1 (epigenetic regulation). Encodes a member of the chromodomain helicase DNA-binding protein family. Members of this family are characterized by a chromodomain, a helicase ATP-binding domain and an additional functional domain.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler CHD5 ([Q8TDI0](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q8TDI0/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** ATP-dependent chromatin-remodeling factor that binds DNA through histones and regulates gene transcription. May specifically recognize and bind trimethylated 'Lys-27' (H3K27me3) and non-methylated 'Lys-4' of histone H3. Acts as a component of the histone deacetylase NuRD complex which participates in the remodeling of chromatin. Plays a role in the development of the nervous system by activating the expression of genes promoting neuron terminal differentiation. In parallel, it may also positively regulate the trimethylation of histone H3 at 'Lys-27' thereby specifically repressing genes that promote the differentiation into non-neuronal cell lineages. Regulates the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation and differentiation. Downstream activated genes may include CDKN2A that positively regulates the p53/TP53 pathway, which in turn, prevents cell proliferation.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Preferentially expressed in total brain, fetal brain, and cerebellum. It is also moderately expressed in the adrenal gland and detected in testis.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Parenti-Mignot neurodevelopmental syndrome (PMNDS).
