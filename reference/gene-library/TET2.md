# TET2: tet methylcytosine dioxygenase 2

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 4 (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene is a methylcytosine dioxygenase that catalyzes the conversion of methylcytosine to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine. The protein is involved in myelopoiesis, and defects in this gene have been associated with several myeloproliferative disorders.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Methylcytosine dioxygenase TET2 ([Q6N021](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q6N021/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Dioxygenase that catalyzes the conversion of the modified genomic base 5-methylcytosine (5mC) into 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC) and plays a key role in active DNA demethylation. Has a preference for 5-hydroxymethylcytosine in CpG motifs. Also mediates subsequent conversion of 5hmC into 5-formylcytosine (5fC), and conversion of 5fC to 5-carboxylcytosine (5caC). Conversion of 5mC into 5hmC, 5fC and 5caC probably constitutes the first step in cytosine demethylation. Methylation at the C5 position of cytosine bases is an epigenetic modification of the mammalian genome which plays an important role in transcriptional regulation. In addition to its role in DNA demethylation, also involved in the recruitment of the O-GlcNAc transferase OGT to CpG-rich transcription start sites of active genes, thereby promoting histone H2B GlcNAcylation by OGT.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Broadly expressed. Highly expressed in hematopoietic cells; highest expression observed in granulocytes. Expression is reduced in granulocytes from peripheral blood of patients affected by myelodysplastic syndromes.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Polycythemia vera (PV); Myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS); Immunodeficiency 75 with lymphoproliferation (IMD75).
