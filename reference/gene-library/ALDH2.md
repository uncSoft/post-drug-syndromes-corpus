# ALDH2: aldehyde dehydrogenase 2 family member

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 12 (drug metabolism (phase i)). This protein belongs to the aldehyde dehydrogenase family of proteins. Aldehyde dehydrogenase is the second enzyme of the major oxidative pathway of alcohol metabolism.

**Corpus relevance:** Phase I enzymes determine exposure to finasteride, SSRIs, and other implicated drugs; variation here shapes the likelihood and persistence of adverse effects.

**Protein (UniProt):** Aldehyde dehydrogenase, mitochondrial ([P05091](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P05091/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Mitochondrial aldehyde dehydrogenase with broad substrate specificity which participates in the detoxification of ethanol-derived acetaldehyde and endogenous aldehydes, including those arising from lipid peroxidation. The reaction mechanism involves a nucleophilic attack on the aldehyde substrate, resulting in formation of a thiohemiacetal intermediate. Subsequent hydride transfer to NAD+, yields a thioester intermediate which is hydrolyzed to the carboxylic acid product through activation of a water molecule by the catalytic glutamate. In vivo, ALDH2 is required for the clearance of formaldehyde, a cytotoxic and carcinogenic endogenous metabolite that induces DNA damage. It also catalyzes the oxidation of 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetaldehyde (DOPAL), a reactive intermediate of dopamine catabolism, to 3,4-dihydroxyphenylacetate (DOPAC).

**Pathway (UniProt):** Alcohol metabolism; ethanol degradation; acetate from ethanol: step 2/2.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Mitochondrion.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** AMED syndrome, digenic (AMEDS).
