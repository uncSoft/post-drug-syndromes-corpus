# PHF8: PHD finger protein 8

A protein-coding gene on chromosome X (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene is a histone lysine demethylase that preferentially acts on histones in the monomethyl or dimethyl states. The protein requires Fe(2+) ion, 2-oxoglutarate, and oxygen for its catalytic activity.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone lysine demethylase PHF8 ([Q9UPP1](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q9UPP1/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Histone lysine demethylase with selectivity for mono- and dimethylated residues. It plays an essential role in cell cycle progression and rDNA transcription. Demethylates mono- and dimethylated histone H3 'Lys-9' residue (H3K9Me1 and H3K9Me2) and monomethylated histone H4 'Lys-20' residue (H4K20Me1). Acts as a transcription activator as H3K9Me1, H3K9Me2, H3K27Me2 and H4K20Me1 are epigenetic repressive marks. Displays a very low intrinsic activity toward dimethylated H3 'Lys-27' (H3K27Me2). May also have weak activity toward dimethylated H3 'Lys-36' (H3K36Me2), however, the relevance of this result remains unsure in vivo. Involved in cell cycle progression by being required to control G1-S transition. Acts as a coactivator of rDNA transcription, by activating polymerase I (pol I) mediated transcription of rRNA genes.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Nucleus, nucleolus.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Intellectual developmental disorder, X-linked, syndromic, Siderius type (MRXSSD).
