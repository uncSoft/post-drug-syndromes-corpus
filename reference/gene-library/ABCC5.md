# ABCC5: ATP binding cassette subfamily C member 5

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 3 (drug and xenobiotic transporters). The protein encoded by this gene is a member of the superfamily of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters. ABC proteins transport various molecules across extra- and intra-cellular membranes.

**Corpus relevance:** Transporters set systemic exposure to finasteride/SSRIs and clear conjugated steroid metabolites, directly relevant to both drug pharmacokinetics and Powers' clearance-centered PFS mechanism.

**Powers note (his unpublished theorizing, research only, not medical advice):** Powers named the ABCC transporter family (2026 summit interview, unpublished) as intracellular transporter genes implicated in metabolite clearance in his experimentally verifiable mechanism. He names ABCC5 itself as the cAMP exporter in that same illustration of transporter overload (PRH-1363).

**Protein (UniProt):** ATP-binding cassette sub-family C member 5 ([O15440](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O15440/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** ATP-dependent transporter of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) family that actively extrudes physiological compounds, and xenobiotics from cells. Mediates ATP-dependent transport of endogenous metabolites such as cAMP and cGMP, folic acid and N-lactoyl-amino acids (in vitro). Also acts as a general glutamate conjugate and analog transporter that can limit the brain levels of endogenous metabolites, drugs, and toxins. Confers resistance to the antiviral agent PMEA. Able to transport several anticancer drugs including methotrexate, and nucleotide analogs in vitro, however it does with low affinity, thus the exact role of ABCC5 in mediating resistance still needs to be elucidated. Acts as a heme transporter required for the translocation of cytosolic heme to the secretory pathway. May play a role in energy metabolism by regulating the glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1) secretion from enteroendocrine cells (By similarity).

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Basolateral cell membrane; Golgi apparatus lumen; Endosome membrane; Cytoplasmic granule; Apical cell membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Ubiquitously expressed, but levels in brain and muscle are especially high. All isoforms are equally expressed in retina.
