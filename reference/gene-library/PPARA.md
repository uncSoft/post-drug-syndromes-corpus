# PPARA: peroxisome proliferator activated receptor alpha

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 22 (steroid and nuclear hormone receptors). Peroxisome proliferators include hypolipidemic drugs, herbicides, leukotriene antagonists, and plasticizers; this term arises because they induce an increase in the size and number of peroxisomes. Peroxisomes are subcellular organelles found in plants and animals that contain enzymes for respiration and for cholesterol and lipid metabolism.

**Corpus relevance:** Nuclear receptors transduce steroid signals into lasting gene-expression programs; persistent receptor-level remodeling (or silencing) is a leading hypothesis for symptoms that outlast drug exposure.

**Protein (UniProt):** Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha ([Q07869](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q07869/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Ligand-activated transcription factor. Key regulator of lipid metabolism. Activated by the endogenous ligand 1-palmitoyl-2-oleoyl-sn-glycerol-3-phosphocholine (16:0/18:1-GPC). Activated by oleylethanolamide, a naturally occurring lipid that regulates satiety. Receptor for peroxisome proliferators such as hypolipidemic drugs and fatty acids. Regulates the peroxisomal beta-oxidation pathway of fatty acids. Functions as a transcription activator for the ACOX1 and P450 genes. Transactivation activity requires heterodimerization with RXRA and is antagonized by NR2C2. May be required for the propagation of clock information to metabolic pathways regulated by PER2.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Skeletal muscle, liver, heart and kidney. Expressed in monocytes.
