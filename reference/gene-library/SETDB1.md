# SETDB1: SET domain bifurcated histone lysine methyltransferase 1

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 1 (epigenetic regulation). Encodes a histone methyltransferase which regulates histone methylation, gene silencing, and transcriptional repression. This gene has been identified as a target for treatment in Huntington Disease, given that gene silencing and transcription dysfunction likely play a role in the disease pathogenesis.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase SETDB1 ([Q15047](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q15047/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Histone methyltransferase that specifically trimethylates 'Lys-9' of histone H3 (H3K9me3). H3 'Lys-9' trimethylation represents a specific tag for epigenetic transcriptional repression by recruiting HP1 (CBX1, CBX3 and/or CBX5) proteins to methylated histones. Mainly functions in euchromatin regions, thereby playing a central role in the silencing of euchromatic genes. H3 'Lys-9' trimethylation is coordinated with DNA methylation. Forms a complex with MBD1 and ATF7IP that represses transcription and couples DNA methylation and histone 'Lys-9' trimethylation. Its activity is dependent on MBD1 and is heritably maintained through DNA replication by being recruited by CAF-1. SETDB1 is targeted to histone H3 by TRIM28/TIF1B, a factor recruited by KRAB zinc-finger proteins. Required for HUSH-mediated heterochromatin formation and gene silencing.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Cytoplasm; Chromosome.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Widely expressed. High expression in testis.
