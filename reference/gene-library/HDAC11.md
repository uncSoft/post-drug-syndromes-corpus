# HDAC11: histone deacetylase 11

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 3 (epigenetic regulation). Encodes a class IV histone deacetylase. The protein is localized to the nucleus and may be involved in regulating the expression of interleukin 10.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone deacetylase 11 ([Q96DB2](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q96DB2/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Responsible for the deacetylation of lysine residues on the N-terminal part of the core histones (H2A, H2B, H3 and H4). Histone deacetylation gives a tag for epigenetic repression and plays an important role in transcriptional regulation, cell cycle progression and developmental events. Histone deacetylases act via the formation of large multiprotein complexes.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Weakly expressed in most tissues. Strongly expressed in brain, heart, skeletal muscle, kidney and testis.
