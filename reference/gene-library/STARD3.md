# STARD3: StAR related lipid transfer domain containing 3

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 17 (steroid hormone synthesis). Encodes a member of a subfamily of lipid trafficking proteins that are characterized by a C-terminal steroidogenic acute regulatory domain and an N-terminal metastatic lymph node 64 domain. The protein localizes to the membranes of late endosomes and may be involved in exporting cholesterol.

**Corpus relevance:** Steroid-synthesis enzymes sit directly on the pathways perturbed by 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors and SSRIs; inherited variation here is a candidate susceptibility factor for persistent post-drug phenotypes.

**Protein (UniProt):** StAR-related lipid transfer protein 3 ([Q14849](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q14849/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Sterol-binding protein that mediates cholesterol transport from the endoplasmic reticulum to endosomes. The sterol transport mechanism is triggered by phosphorylation of FFAT motif that leads to membrane tethering between the endoplasmic reticulum and late endosomes via interaction with VAPA and VAPB. Acts as a lipid transfer protein that redirects sterol to the endosome at the expense of the cell membrane and favors membrane formation inside endosomes. May also mediate cholesterol transport between other membranes, such as mitochondria membrane or cell membrane. However, such results need additional experimental evidence; probably mainly mediates cholesterol transport from the endoplasmic reticulum to endosomes. Does not activate transcriptional cholesterol sensing. Able to bind other lipids, such as lutein, a xanthophyll carotenoids that form the macular pigment of the retina.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Late endosome membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Expressed in retina.
