# ABCC4: ATP binding cassette subfamily C member 4 (PEL blood group)

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 13 (drug and xenobiotic transporters). The protein encoded by this gene is a member of the superfamily of ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters. ABC proteins transport various molecules across extra- and intra-cellular membranes.

**Corpus relevance:** Transporters set systemic exposure to finasteride/SSRIs and clear conjugated steroid metabolites, directly relevant to both drug pharmacokinetics and Powers' clearance-centered PFS mechanism.

**Powers note (his unpublished theorizing, research only, not medical advice):** Powers named the ABCC transporter family (2026 summit interview, unpublished) as intracellular transporter genes implicated in metabolite clearance in his experimentally verifiable mechanism; individual ABCC members not separately named.

**Protein (UniProt):** ATP-binding cassette sub-family C member 4 ([O15439](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O15439/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** ATP-dependent transporter of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) family that actively extrudes physiological compounds and xenobiotics from cells. Transports a range of endogenous molecules that have a key role in cellular communication and signaling, including cyclic nucleotides such as cyclic AMP (cAMP) and cyclic GMP (cGMP), bile acids, steroid conjugates, urate, and prostaglandins. Mediates the ATP-dependent efflux of glutathione conjugates such as leukotriene C4 (LTC4) and leukotriene B4 (LTB4) too. The presence of GSH is necessary for the ATP-dependent transport of LTB4, whereas GSH is not required for the transport of LTC4. Mediates the cotransport of bile acids with reduced glutathione (GSH). May contribute to hepatic, intestinal and renal excretion of steroid conjugates. Selectively transports steroid glucuronates and sulfates. Mediates the efflux of estrogens glucuronated at C-16 and C-17 positions, but has no transporter activity toward estrogens glucuronated at C-3.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Basolateral cell membrane; Apical cell membrane.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Widely expressed, with particularly high levels in prostate, but is barely detectable in liver. sinusoidal membrane of hepatocytes.
