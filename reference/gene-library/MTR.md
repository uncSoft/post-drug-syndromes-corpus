# MTR: 5-methyltetrahydrofolate-homocysteine methyltransferase

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 1 (methylation and one-carbon metabolism). Encodes the 5-methyltetrahydrofolate-homocysteine methyltransferase. This enzyme, also known as cobalamin-dependent methionine synthase, catalyzes the final step in methionine biosynthesis.

**Corpus relevance:** One-carbon metabolism supplies methyl groups for DNA/histone methylation, the epigenetic layer implicated in persistent post-drug changes.

**Protein (UniProt):** Methionine synthase ([Q99707](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q99707/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from methylcob(III)alamin (MeCbl) to homocysteine, yielding enzyme-bound cob(I)alamin and methionine in the cytosol. MeCbl is an active form of cobalamin (vitamin B12) used as a cofactor for methionine biosynthesis. Cob(I)alamin form is regenerated to MeCbl by a transfer of a methyl group from 5-methyltetrahydrofolate. The processing of cobalamin in the cytosol occurs in a multiprotein complex composed of at least MMACHC, MMADHC, MTRR (methionine synthase reductase) and MTR which may contribute to shuttle safely and efficiently cobalamin towards MTR in order to produce methionine.

**Pathway (UniProt):** Amino-acid biosynthesis; L-methionine biosynthesis via de novo pathway; L-methionine from L-homocysteine (MetH route): step 1/1.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cytoplasm.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Widely expressed. Expressed at the highest levels in pancreas, heart, brain, skeletal muscle and placenta. Expressed at lower levels in lung, liver and kidney.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Homocystinuria-megaloblastic anemia, cblG type (HMAG); Neural tube defects, folate-sensitive (NTDFS).
