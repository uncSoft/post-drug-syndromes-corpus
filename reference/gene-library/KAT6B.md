# KAT6B: lysine acetyltransferase 6B

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 10 (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene is a histone acetyltransferase and component of the MOZ/MORF protein complex. In addition to its acetyltransferase activity, the encoded protein has transcriptional activation activity in its N-terminal end and transcriptional repression activity in its C-terminal end.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone acetyltransferase KAT6B ([Q8WYB5](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q8WYB5/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Histone acetyltransferase which may be involved in both positive and negative regulation of transcription. Required for RUNX2-dependent transcriptional activation. May be involved in cerebral cortex development. Component of the MOZ/MORF complex which has a histone H3 acetyltransferase activity.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Ubiquitously expressed, with high levels in heart, pancreas, testis and ovary.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Ohdo syndrome, SBBYS variant (SBBYSS); Genitopatellar syndrome (GTPTS).
