# TH: tyrosine hydroxylase

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 11 (neurotransmission). The protein encoded by this gene is involved in the conversion of tyrosine to dopamine. It is the rate-limiting enzyme in the synthesis of catecholamines, hence plays a key role in the physiology of adrenergic neurons.

**Corpus relevance:** Monoamine, GABA, glutamate, and cholinergic systems are the direct pharmacologic targets of SSRIs/SNRIs (PSSD) and the downstream effectors of neurosteroid signaling (PFS).

**Protein (UniProt):** Tyrosine 3-monooxygenase ([P07101](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P07101/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt):** Catalyzes the conversion of L-tyrosine to L-dihydroxyphenylalanine (L-Dopa), the rate-limiting step in the biosynthesis of catecholamines, dopamine, noradrenaline, and adrenaline. Uses tetrahydrobiopterin and molecular oxygen to convert tyrosine to L-Dopa. In addition to tyrosine, is able to catalyze the hydroxylation of phenylalanine and tryptophan with lower specificity (By similarity). Positively regulates the regression of retinal hyaloid vessels during postnatal development (By similarity).

**Pathway (UniProt):** Catecholamine biosynthesis; dopamine biosynthesis; dopamine from L-tyrosine: step 1/2.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Cytoplasm, perinuclear region; Nucleus; Cell projection, axon; Cytoplasm; Cytoplasmic vesicle, secretory vesicle, synaptic vesicle.

**Tissue specificity (UniProt):** Mainly expressed in the brain and adrenal glands.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Segawa syndrome autosomal recessive (ARSEGS).
