# SUV39H1: SUV39H1 histone lysine methyltransferase

A protein-coding gene on chromosome X (epigenetic regulation). Encodes an evolutionarily-conserved protein containing an N-terminal chromodomain and a C-terminal SET domain. The protein is a histone methyltransferase that trimethylates lysine 9 of histone H3, which results in transcriptional gene silencing.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone-lysine N-methyltransferase SUV39H1 ([O43463](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/O43463/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Histone methyltransferase that specifically mediates trimethylation of 'Lys-9' of histone H3 (H3K9me3) using monomethylated H3 'Lys-9' (H3K9me1) as substrate. Also weakly methylates histone H1 (in vitro). H3 'Lys-9' trimethylation represents a specific tag for epigenetic transcriptional repression by recruiting HP1 (CBX1, CBX3 and/or CBX5) proteins to methylated histones. Mainly functions in heterochromatin regions, thereby playing a central role in the establishment of constitutive heterochromatin at pericentric and telomere regions (By similarity). H3 'Lys-9' trimethylation is also required to direct DNA methylation at pericentric repeats.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome; Nucleus lamina; Nucleus, nucleoplasm; Chromosome, centromere; Cytoplasmic vesicle, phagosome lumen; Cell membrane.
