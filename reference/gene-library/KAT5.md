# KAT5: lysine acetyltransferase 5

A protein-coding gene on chromosome 11 (epigenetic regulation). The protein encoded by this gene belongs to the MYST family of histone acetyl transferases (HATs) and was originally isolated as an HIV-1 TAT-interactive protein. HATs play important roles in regulating chromatin remodeling, transcription and other nuclear processes by acetylating histone and nonhistone proteins.

**Corpus relevance:** Epigenetic persistence is one proposed explanation for why post-drug syndromes endure after the drug is gone, and no study in the library shows an epigenetic change outlasting the drug that caused it; chromatin regulators are candidates for a mechanism that is still unproven.

**Protein (UniProt):** Histone acetyltransferase KAT5 ([Q92993](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q92993/entry), reviewed)

**Function (UniProt, excerpt):** Catalytic subunit of the NuA4 histone acetyltransferase complex, a multiprotein complex involved in transcriptional activation of select genes principally by acetylation of nucleosomal histones H2A and H4. Histone acetylation alters nucleosome-DNA interactions and promotes interaction of the modified histones with other proteins which positively regulate transcription. The NuA4 histone acetyltransferase complex is required for the activation of transcriptional programs associated with proto-oncogene mediated growth induction, tumor suppressor mediated growth arrest and replicative senescence, apoptosis, and DNA repair.

**Subcellular location (UniProt):** Nucleus; Chromosome; Cytoplasm; Chromosome, centromere, kinetochore; Cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, spindle pole; Nucleus, nucleolus; Cytoplasm, perinuclear region.

**Associated conditions (UniProt):** Neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies, sleep disturbance, and brain abnormalities (NEDFASB).
