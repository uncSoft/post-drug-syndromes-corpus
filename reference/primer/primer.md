# Post-Finasteride Syndrome and Post-SSRI Sexual Dysfunction: A Primer

## About this primer

This primer summarizes the library's records on post-finasteride syndrome (PFS), post-SSRI sexual dysfunction (PSSD) and a related post-retinoid condition, and gives no medical advice. Dr Will Powers' ideas are his own unpublished theorizing, and he says himself that parts lack direct evidence [DWP-010]. Evidence levels are flagged throughout.

It is a living document, revised when the library takes in work that changes what can be said rather than on a schedule. The reference list is rebuilt from the library every time the site is built, so each reference carries its record's current title, byline and status, and a retracted or withdrawn source is marked as such wherever it appears. Last revised 1 November 2026, against Corpus v1.48.

## What PFS and PSSD are

### Definitions and symptoms

Post-finasteride syndrome (PFS) means sexual symptoms, often with mental and physical ones, that continue after someone stops finasteride [CROSS-002, LIT-004], a drug that blocks the enzyme 5α-reductase and is taken for male pattern hair loss or an enlarged prostate [LIT-006, LIT-011]. Post-SSRI sexual dysfunction (PSSD) is sexual dysfunction that continues after stopping a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) or related antidepressant, drugs that act on the brain chemical serotonin [CROSS-002, LIT-023].

The main symptoms fall into four groups:

- **Sexual:** low or lost desire, erectile dysfunction, weak or pleasureless orgasm [CROSS-001].
- **Genital sensation:** numbness (genital anesthesia) appeared in all three drug groups of a self-selected 300-case series of reports to a drug-safety website [CROSS-001]; 87% of 79 men with lasting symptoms after finasteride, 62 recruited from an online PFS forum, reported lost penile sensitivity a mean of 44 months after stopping [LIT-004].
- **Mental and cognitive:** loss of pleasure (anhedonia), poor concentration and anxiety; 76% of those 79 men reported anhedonia [LIT-004].
- **Physical:** fatigue in 86 of 129 men (67%) in a Polish study of men who already had symptoms [LIT-006]; muscle stiffness, cramps and tremor in all 3 men of one small Milan study [PFS-004].

Persistence is the defining feature: in the 300-case series, 17 people reported symptoms lasting at least 10 years, and for about 1 in 5 the problems began or worsened only after stopping [CROSS-001]. Reports come mostly from men [CROSS-001], and for finasteride the signal is concentrated in young men treated for hair loss [LIT-077].

Consensus criteria published in 2022 by 37 authors, drafted from two case collections from a drug-safety website, require prior exposure to the drug class and an enduring change after stopping: in genital sensation for PSSD, in sexual function for PFS; they have not been tested for reliability or validity in patients [CROSS-002]. PSSD has no code in ICD-11 or DSM-5, the main diagnostic manuals [CUR-006].

### What the names leave out

Both names single out sex, and the consensus criteria follow them: they require an enduring change in genital sensation or sexual function after stopping, and treat non-sexual symptoms such as emotional blunting and cognitive impairment as variable accompaniments rather than as part of the definition [CROSS-002].

What people report does not divide that way. Among the 79 men with lasting symptoms after finasteride, 76% reported anhedonia, a loss of the capacity for pleasure, alongside the 87% who reported lost penile sensitivity [LIT-004]; two thirds of 129 men in a Polish series reported fatigue [LIT-006]; and clinicians managing PSSD describe marked distress and poor quality of life as the ordinary picture rather than an occasional complication [LIT-030]. The mental symptoms are not simply depression returning: in a survey of 532 people, genital anesthesia was independent of depression and anxiety, although depression also predicted how severe current symptoms were [PSSD-006]. Regulators have acted on the non-sexual symptoms in their own right, the EU confirming suicidal ideation as a side effect of finasteride [LIT-066] and the UK stating that finasteride is associated with depression, suicidal ideation and sexual dysfunction that may persist after treatment stops [LIT-067].

A name shapes what gets measured. Neither syndrome has a code in ICD-11 or DSM-5 [CUR-006], the criteria that exist have not been tested in patients [CROSS-002], and an instrument built to score sexual function will not register anhedonia or cognitive change however disabling they are. This primer and the library use PFS and PSSD throughout, because they are the terms the literature and the regulators use, but the names describe the part of the problem that was noticed first rather than its boundaries.

### Who reports it, and who is missing

Almost everything measured so far was measured in men, most of it in young men treated for hair loss [LIT-004, LIT-077]. That is a fact about who has been studied, not about who can be affected.

**Women.** Of the 300 cases in the largest series, 55 were women [CROSS-001]. A 2019 review of post-SSRI sexual dysfunction found no clear definition in the literature and very few data on differences between women and men, and called for instruments covering all sexual complaints, genital anesthesia and pleasureless orgasm among them, in both [CUR-059]. Every spinal-fluid study in the library is in men [PFS-004, PFS-005, DISC-013], the withdrawal experiments are almost all in male rats [RES-003, DISC-012], and the one abstract in female rats calls them a neglected sex in this research [DISC-016]. The enzyme at the center of the biology is not male-specific: a meta-analysis of five studies, 356 women with polycystic ovary syndrome against 236 controls, found higher 5α-reductase activity on two urinary ratios, with substantial disagreement between studies on one of them [CUR-109].

**Trans and gender-diverse people.** The exposure is not confined to cisgender men. The library holds a 2020 post in which Dr Powers asked a subreddit of transgender patients which of them had developed post-finasteride syndrome after finasteride or dutasteride, saying he had a theory as to why it seemed to happen in trans women more often than in cis men [DWP-001]. That is an uncontrolled clinical impression from an unpublished source, and the record notes that no study in the library tests how often PFS occurs in transgender people [DWP-001]. The one study here that sampled a sexual and gender minority population reports the highest proportion the library holds for any group of past users: 13.2% of 707 past antidepressant users reported persistent genital numbness, against 1 of 102 past users of other psychiatric medicines, among 2,179 people aged 15 to 29, a group in which many had had hormone therapy [CUR-007]. It is self-reported and self-selected, so it cannot give a rate [CUR-007].

No study in the library compares how often these syndromes occur between women and men, or between cisgender and transgender people, which is why none of the figures above can be read as a rate for anyone. Absence of data is not absence of illness, and the people least represented in this literature are the ones whose clinicians have the least to go on.

### Regulatory recognition

- 2008 to 2009: Sweden and the UK first warned of persistent sexual effects of finasteride, by one opinion article's account [LIT-003].
- 2011 to 2012: US finasteride labels added erectile dysfunction continuing after discontinuation [LIT-072].
- 2019: the EU's drug-safety committee recommended that six SSRIs and four related antidepressants warn of long-lasting sexual dysfunction reported after stopping [CUR-048].
- 2021: Health Canada reviewed 58 reports of sexual dysfunction after stopping SSRIs or related antidepressants, judged 43 possibly linked to the drugs, and could neither confirm nor rule out a causal link [LIT-071].
- 2022: France required a red warning on low-dose finasteride packs [LIT-069]; the US FDA added suicidal ideation to the label but judged causation of persistent sexual problems unestablished [LIT-072].
- 2025 and 2026: the EU confirmed suicidal ideation as a finasteride side effect [LIT-066]; the UK said finasteride's sexual dysfunction may persist after stopping [LIT-067] and will name PSSD in the leaflets of ten antidepressants [CUR-006].

### A related condition: post-retinoid

Post-retinoid sexual dysfunction (PRSD) has been described after isotretinoin, an acne drug; the 2022 criteria include it [CROSS-002], and 54 of the 300 cases in the largest series followed isotretinoin [CROSS-001]. A systematic review of 11 studies called the evidence conflicting and of low quality, with most studies describing effects during treatment [LIT-037], and a scoping review of 55 papers judged the evidence for a link very weak [LIT-038]. The UK regulator kept sexual-function questions in its 2026 isotretinoin measures because such effects have been reported to persist [CUR-033].

## The biology in brief

The enzyme 5α-reductase turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a stronger form of testosterone [LIT-011], and starts the production of allopregnanolone, a brain steroid, from progesterone [MECH-007]. It has three versions, types 1, 2 and 3, found in many organs including the brain [LIT-008, LIT-011]. In the prefrontal cortex of male rats, type 1 sustains baseline allopregnanolone and type 2 is recruited under acute stress [MECH-008]. Finasteride blocks mainly type 2 [LIT-011]; dutasteride, a related drug, blocks both type 1 and type 2 [CUR-046, MED-002].

Neurosteroids are steroids that tune nerve-cell signaling; many act on the GABA-A receptor, the brain's main inhibitory receptor [GL-gaba-a-receptor], boosting the effect of the messenger GABA and activating the receptor directly [DISC-002]. Allopregnanolone and THDOC (tetrahydrodeoxycorticosterone) are made from progesterone and deoxycorticosterone by 5α-reductase and then a 3α-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (3α-HSD, enzymes of the AKR1C family) [MECH-007, CUR-032]; the same step turns DHT into 3α-androstanediol [CUR-032]. In mouse and rat brain, type 1 5α-reductase and 3α-HSD sit together in nerve cells, so the brain can make these steroids itself [MECH-007]. In stressed rats, finasteride lowered brain levels of several neurosteroids, allopregnanolone among them [LIT-052].

Steroids are cleared partly by attaching a sugar group (glucuronidation) or a sulfate group (sulfation) [MECH-049, MECH-019]. The enzymes UGT2B15 and UGT2B17 glucuronidate DHT and its metabolites, such as 3α-androstanediol [MECH-049]; gut bacterial enzymes can cut the sugar off again, shown in test-tube experiments for estrogens [MECH-016] and, in a conference abstract, for testosterone [MECH-014]. In healthy volunteers given testosterone, those with fewer copies of the sulfation gene SULT2A1 excreted less sulfated androgen [MECH-019].

The androgen receptor is the protein through which testosterone and DHT act [SIDE-009]; its gene has CAG and GGN repeats of variable length [SIDE-011]. Reviews propose that one serotonin receptor, 5-HT1A, becomes less responsive in PSSD, an idea one review calls unproven [PSSD-003, CUR-055]. The drug classes also meet in the steroid chain: in test-tube experiments with purified enzymes, three SSRIs made the step that produces allopregnanolone 10 to 18 times more efficient, with nothing measured in people, and finasteride blocks the step before it [CUR-032].

### Key terms

- **5α-reductase:** enzyme that makes DHT [LIT-011].
- **DHT:** dihydrotestosterone, a stronger form of testosterone [PFS-004].
- **Neurosteroid:** steroid that tunes nerve-cell signaling [DISC-002].
- **Allopregnanolone:** neurosteroid made from progesterone that boosts GABA-A receptors [MECH-007, DISC-002].
- **THDOC:** stress-linked neurosteroid that boosts GABA-A receptors [MECH-001].
- **3α-androstanediol:** steroid made from DHT [PFS-005].
- **GABA-A receptor:** the brain's main inhibitory receptor [GL-gaba-a-receptor].
- **Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF):** fluid around the brain and spinal cord, sampled by lumbar puncture [DISC-013, PFS-004].

## What has been found

Many finasteride studies below were funded at least in part by the Post-Finasteride Syndrome Foundation [PFS-004, PFS-005, DISC-013, SIDE-009, SIDE-010, LIT-009, LIT-002, DISC-008, DISC-025, RES-003, DISC-012, LIT-015].

### Clinical picture and frequency

The 300-case series gathered reports sent to the authors' own drug-safety website: 221 after antidepressants, 54 after isotretinoin and 25 after finasteride-type drugs [CROSS-001]. A self-selected online survey of 827 people, recruited through support forums and social media (conference abstract), found overlapping symptoms, though sexual dysfunction was less common after isotretinoin [CUR-030]. Neither has a comparison group, so neither can show frequency or cause [CROSS-001, CUR-030].

Drug-safety monitoring (pharmacovigilance) relies on spontaneous reports; one report does not prove a cause, and because neither the number of reactions nor the number of users is known, reports cannot give rates [CUR-050]. In the US FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), 577 of 4,910 reports for men aged 18 to 45 on low-dose finasteride described persistent sexual dysfunction [LIT-017]. EU reviewers read 123 case narratives of possibly persistent sexual dysfunction on antidepressants from EudraVigilance, the EU's adverse-reaction database [CUR-049]. They rated none above possibly related, because drug and illness were hard to separate, although 84 of them came from patients, a level of concern they judged to warrant some mitigation [CUR-049]. The Dutch pharmacovigilance center Lareb received 86 reports of persistent sexual dysfunction after SSRIs by 2021, one lasting 23 years [CUR-052].

In one Chicago health system's records, erectile dysfunction treated with an erection drug and still noted at least 90 days after stopping appeared in 1.4% of 11,909 men exposed to finasteride or dutasteride; with no unexposed group, this is not a rate attributable to the drugs [LIT-002]. Four of 866 Israeli men (0.46%) who had taken a serotonergic antidepressant met a narrow definition based on treated erectile dysfunction [LIT-023]. In a self-reported survey of sexual and gender minority youth aged 15 to 29, 13.2% of 707 past antidepressant users reported persistent genital numbness, against 1 of 102 past users of other psychiatric medicines [CUR-007].

### Measurements in patients

Three studies from one Milan group, of 3, 7 and 14 men, found lower DHT, lower progesterone-derived steroids and higher testosterone in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of men who had stopped finasteride than in control samples [PFS-004, PFS-005, DISC-013]. The patients may overlap; the 2013 controls were samples from diagnostic lumbar punctures [PFS-004], the later controls people having spinal anesthesia for orthopedic surgery [PFS-005, DISC-013]. No samples predated the drug, findings differ between studies (CSF pregnenolone, a precursor steroid, was higher in 2015 and lower in 2017), and the library has not yet checked a 2023 correction to the 2015 paper [PFS-005, DISC-013].

In a Boston study, blood testosterone and DHT in 25 men with persistent symptoms did not differ significantly from those of 13 former users without symptoms and 18 never-users, and were in the normal range, despite markedly worse sexual function [SIDE-010].

An earlier series of 24 otherwise healthy young men with persistent symptoms after low-dose finasteride found average testosterone and DHT within normal ranges as well, but 13% had confirmed low levels of both, above the roughly 5% expected in the general population; semen analysis in the same men found severe oligospermia in 16%, low sperm motility in 44% and abnormal morphology in 50%, with no pre-treatment samples to compare against [SIDE-008].

A Baylor study of penile skin from 26 men with PFS found 1,446 genes more active, including the androgen receptor gene, and 2,318 less active than in foreskin from 26 men having circumcision, while two blood markers of receptor activity did not differ; the tissue sites differed and no samples predated the drug [SIDE-009].

A pilot of 21 men with PFS found lower gut-bacterial richness on two of five measures than in 10 healthy men from an earlier study, who were about 12 years older [DISC-008]. In CSF DNA, a methylation mark (a chemical tag that can turn a gene down) was found on SRD5A2, the gene for type 2 5α-reductase, in 9 of 16 men with PFS and 1 of 13 controls, and in no one's blood; it is unknown whether it predated finasteride [DISC-025].

Nerve findings are mixed: recordings of the pudendal nerve, which carries feeling from the penis, were abnormal in 4 of 16 men after finasteride [DISC-013]. At the Queen Square uro-neurology unit, 9 of 55 patients with genital sensory impairment met the PSSD criteria; all 9 had an abnormal sensory examination, a rate that did not differ significantly from patients with other causes; evoked potentials (recordings of nerve signals reaching the brain) were normal in 8, and the authors proposed a central cause and did not test small fibers (conference abstract) [CUR-003]. A single-center chart review of 43 men with PSSD (conference abstract) reported sensory testing consistent with a peripheral component [LIT-033]; corneal microscopy, an eye scan of small nerve fibers, showed peripheral neuropathy in two of three men with post-drug sexual dysfunction in an uncontrolled case series [LIT-040].

### Animal studies

In the Milan design, male rats receive finasteride for 20 days and are followed for a month: the cortex then held lower neurosteroid levels (8 rats per group), more androgen receptor and lower activity of two GABA-A receptor genes (5 per group) [RES-003]. Rats showed depressive-like behavior, fewer dividing brain cells and altered gut bacteria [LIT-049], anxiety-like behavior [LIT-015], and inflammation in the colon and the hypothalamus, a hormone-control region of the brain, that allopregnanolone partly reversed [DISC-009, DISC-010]. Other results point the opposite way: in the same design, no single gene in the hypothalamus or hippocampus passed the cutoff a month after stopping (4 rats per group), though some hippocampal gene-set changes remained [DISC-012], and in mice suppressed growth of new brain cells recovered by 35 days after a 7-day course [LIT-014].

In male rats given paroxetine for two weeks, brain neurosteroids changed region by region, with a different, mostly lower pattern a month later; plasma never changed and no behavior was tested [PSSD-009]. In the nucleus accumbens, a reward-related region, 245 genes changed on treatment and 6 were still changed a month after the last dose [PSSD-012]. The female side of this work is one congress abstract: after two weeks of paroxetine, neurosteroid-synthesis genes in the nucleus accumbens of female rats were altered at the end of treatment, and four of them were still reduced a month after withdrawal [DISC-016]. These studies cannot show that people's symptoms arise the same way [RES-003, PSSD-009].

### Evidence against or complicating

In the Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial, 18,880 men aged 55 or over were randomized to finasteride or placebo for seven years; over a median 20 years, US Medicare insurance claims for 14,239 of them showed no difference reaching the authors' threshold (p < 0.01) in any of 22 proposed post-finasteride conditions; the nearest were Peyronie's disease, less common on finasteride (p = 0.02), and decreased libido, more common (p = 0.11) [CUR-010]. An earlier analysis of the same trial found slightly more depression among former finasteride users [CUR-010]. The men were older and took the prostate dose, outcomes were billing-coded diagnoses, and 68% of the finasteride arm were still taking the drug when claims began [CUR-010].

A pooled analysis of nine observational studies of 5α-reductase inhibitor users (men with prostate enlargement or hair loss) found no statistically significant association with suicide or neuropsychiatric events, with a wide margin of error and substantial differences between studies; the mixed populations limit what it says about PFS [PFS-012].

A nocebo effect, in which symptoms arise from expectation, has been argued: finasteride drew more US sexual reports than dutasteride, a more potent inhibitor, and reports of both rose from 2012, the year the Post-Finasteride Syndrome Foundation began raising awareness [CUR-015]. The authors of a WHO database study saw a similar pattern and judged it unlikely to be fully explained by bias [LIT-077].

Depression and anxiety are hard to separate from the drugs in many studies: a 2023 systematic review of 19 studies of SSRIs, mostly at high risk of bias and with little control for psychiatric illness and other conditions, could make no reliable prevalence estimate of persistent sexual dysfunction and could not establish a cause-effect relationship, noting that persisting dysfunction may be linked to relapse of depression or anxiety, though continued disturbance could not be entirely ruled out [LIT-025]. A 2015 survey of 532 people found genital anesthesia independent of depression and anxiety, though depression also predicted current severity [PSSD-006].

The Milan group's review lists neuroactive steroids, serotonin and dopamine signaling, and gut bacteria among mechanisms PFS and PSSD may share [PFS-003]. Dr Will Powers, a family physician who says he treats people with PFS and PSSD [PRH-0402, YT-3g52vlv5YWo], has posted on Reddit a model that links steroid handling, gene variants and tissue feedback; it is his own, unpublished theorizing, and he says himself that parts of it lack direct evidence [DWP-010]. In his September 2026 statement he proposes that finasteride or an SSRI changes how 5α- and 3α-reduced steroids are made, broken down and moved in and out of cells; that gene variants, for example in glucuronidation and sulfation, raise risk without guaranteeing it [DWP-010, DWP-003]; that tissue-level feedback loops keep the abnormal handling going after the drug stops; and that symptoms differ by tissue, whether brain, genital tissue, nerve or skin [DWP-010]. For at least one subgroup, with low libido and loss of pleasure, he proposes an excess of GABA-A-active steroid, THDOC being his leading candidate, a reversal of his first model, a deficiency of allopregnanolone [DWP-010, DWP-002, DWP-001].

## Contradictions and open questions

- **Too little or too much neurosteroid signaling.** The Milan CSF studies found lower progesterone-derived steroids on the allopregnanolone pathway but higher 3α-androstanediol [PFS-005, DISC-013]; Dr Powers proposes an excess of GABA-A-active steroid in at least one subgroup, with THDOC his leading candidate and 3α-androstanediol among his alternatives [DWP-010].
- **Why symptoms persist after the drug is gone.** The finasteride-enzyme complex has a half-life of about 31 days [LIT-011], yet symptoms are reported years after stopping [LIT-004]. Epigenetic change is a proposed explanation, but the one methylation study cannot say whether it preceded the drug [DISC-025].
- **Why only some people.** Extreme androgen receptor repeats were more common in men with persistent effects, and in untreated men with hair loss, than in men without hair loss [SIDE-011], but a three-group study found no explanatory variants [SIDE-010]. Variants in the liver enzymes CYP3A4 and CYP3A5 were linked to finasteride blood levels in a prostate cancer trial, but no study in the library relates them to persistent symptoms [MECH-043].
- **One shared pathway or several.** Symptoms overlap across the three drug groups [CROSS-001, CUR-030], and in test-tube experiments with purified enzymes SSRIs sped up the step after the one finasteride blocks, with nothing measured in people [CUR-032]. But no study in the library measures hormones, neurosteroids or epigenetic marks in people with persistent symptoms after isotretinoin; the nearest, a 2026 preprint, compared reproductive hormones by prescription records without studying sexual function [LIT-041].
- **How common.** The figures above rest on different definitions and populations [LIT-023, CUR-007], and a 2026 consensus review calls the rate of PSSD unknown, yet calls it too rare for feasible prospective studies [CUR-054].
- **Blood versus brain.** DHT was lower in CSF and unchanged in plasma in the Milan studies of 7 and 14 men [PFS-005, DISC-013]; in rats it was lower in plasma but not in CSF or brain the day after the last dose, and lower in plasma, CSF and cerebellum a month later [RES-003]. Dr Powers cautions that CSF levels reflect clearance as well as synthesis [DWP-010].
- **Animal model validity.** The withdrawal studies mostly come from one laboratory group, in male rats, with 4 to 8 animals per group for molecular work and one follow-up a month after the last dose [DISC-012, RES-003, LIT-049, LIT-015]; one other group followed rats for four weeks after stopping and found brain type 2 5α-reductase still suppressed [CUR-011]. In two abstracts, six weeks of finasteride eased depression-like behavior in obese rats while lean rats showed it on the drug and after withdrawal [CUR-079], and a six-week course and a two-week course followed by four weeks off both improved an anxiety-like measure in early-aging rats [CUR-080].
- **Who is affected, by sex and by gender.** Patient data come mostly from men: 55 of the 300 cases were women [CROSS-001], a 2019 review found very few data on differences between the sexes [CUR-059], the spinal fluid studies are all in men [PFS-004, PFS-005, DISC-013], the animal work is almost all in males with one abstract in females [DISC-016], and no study in the library measures neurosteroids in people with PSSD of either sex [PSSD-009]. For transgender people, who take these drugs too, the library has one clinician's unpublished impression and no study at all [DWP-001].

## What would settle them

- **Prospective cohorts** of young people starting finasteride, an SSRI or isotretinoin, with validated sexual-function and mood questionnaires before, during and for years after stopping, against people with the same condition not taking the drug [CUR-014, CUR-026], and studies of primary-care records on how often PSSD occurs and lasts, as the UK regulator proposed [CUR-006]. Enrolling women and transgender people on these drugs would settle a separate question, since neither group appears in any measurement study here [CUR-059, DWP-001].
- **Stored samples in those cohorts:** a full steroid panel in blood, plus stored blood and, where feasible, other tissue for methylation tests (the SRD5A2 mark was found in spinal-fluid DNA but in no blood sample), comparing people who do and do not develop persistent symptoms [DISC-025, DISC-013].
- **A CSF panel** that includes deoxycorticosterone and THDOC, in symptomatic former users, symptom-free former users and never-users, with independent replication of the Milan studies [DISC-013, DWP-010].
- **Nerve testing** with corneal microscopy, thermal thresholds and evoked potentials in people with persistent genital numbness after each drug class, people numb from other causes, and controls [CUR-003, LIT-040, LIT-033].
- **Genetics, gut and animals:** baseline genotyping of men starting finasteride (androgen receptor repeats, CYP3A4/5, UGT2B17, SULT2A1) [SIDE-011, MECH-043, MECH-038, MECH-019]; stool sequencing with gut transit time alongside serum steroids [DISC-008, SIDE-005]; rodent work in both sexes from independent laboratories, with drug-free periods of months [PSSD-012, DISC-012].

For PSSD, treatment reports are limited to small uncontrolled series and single cases [LIT-025], and a 2026 consensus review states that no treatment recommendations are established [CUR-054].
